# Vision

## Purpose

Most people's savings are spread across several places — a bank savings account here, a
fund portfolio there — and no single view tells them how they are actually doing. Banks
show balances; they do not show whether you are saving at the rate you intended, and they
certainly do not show it together with the people you are saving *with*.

financial-tracker is a place to write that down and see it in one picture: what you have,
what you put in, and whether you are keeping to the pace you set yourself — alone or as a
group.

## Who it is for

- **Individuals** who want a manual, honest overview of their savings across institutions.
- **Groups saving toward something shared** — a couple saving for a house, friends saving
  for a trip, a family building a buffer — who want to see joint progress.

## What it is not

- **It never touches money.** No transfers, no payments, no bank connections. Every figure
  is entered by a user. This is a hard product boundary, not a v1 limitation.
- **Not an advisor.** It reports what the user entered; it does not recommend investments.
- **Not an accounting or budgeting tool.** It tracks savings and portfolio value, not
  day-to-day spending.

## Principles

1. **Contribution and performance are different things.** What you put aside is your
   effort; what the market did is not. The application keeps them visibly separate.
2. **Nothing is guessed.** Every number shown is either entered by a user or derived from
   entered data by a rule that can be explained.
3. **Sharing is explicit.** Data is private until a user joins a group, and what joining
   reveals is stated plainly before they accept.
4. **Simple enough to finish.** A learning project with several collaborators: prefer the
   conventional solution and a working feature over an elegant unfinished one.

## What success looks like

A user can, in under a minute a month, record what they set aside and what their portfolio
is worth — and see at a glance whether they and their group are on pace.
